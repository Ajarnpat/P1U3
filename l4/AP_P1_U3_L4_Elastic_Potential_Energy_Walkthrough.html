1 · Quick review: ideal springs

An ideal spring has negligible mass and exerts a restoring force proportional to displacement from its relaxed length.

F⃗s = −k Δx⃗
Direction matters. The minus sign means the spring force points toward equilibrium.
Quick check: If k doubles but the extension is unchanged, what happens to the magnitude of the spring force?

2 · Spring experiment: build the F–Δx graph

Imagine increasing the applied force slowly, so the spring is in equilibrium at each step. The applied force magnitude is equal to kΔx. Add one force step at a time and record the values in Q4–Q5 of the worksheet.

Hidden model: k = 200 N m⁻¹. Force steps are 0, 10, 20, …, 60 N.

stepF / NΔx / m
Quick check: What does the gradient of the F-versus-Δx graph represent?
The gradient is the spring constant k, with units N m⁻¹.

3 · Variable force: work is area

AP Physics 1 treats work by a variable parallel force as the signed area under an F∥-versus-displacement graph. For slow stretching, the applied force rises linearly from zero.

Won spring = area under Fapplied–Δx graph

The graph is a triangle:

Won spring = ½ × Δx × F

and because F = kΔx:

Won spring = ½k(Δx)²
Quick check: A spring reaches F = 40 N at Δx = 0.20 m. What is the area under the graph? J

4 · Derive the stored elastic energy

  1. Stretch the spring slowly from the relaxed length.
  2. The applied force is equal in magnitude to the spring force at each displacement.
  3. The work done on the spring is the triangular area under the graph.
  4. For an ideal spring, that work is stored reversibly as elastic potential energy.
Us = ½k(Δx)²
Zero choice: we normally choose Us = 0 at the relaxed length. Us is positive for both extension and compression because Δx is squared.

5 · Use Us correctly

A. From relaxed length

Us = ½k(Δx)²

B. Between two nonzero displacements

ΔUs = ½k(Δxf)² − ½k(Δxi)²
Common error: do not use ½k(Δxf−Δxi)².

C. Compression

If the spring is compressed, Δx may be negative, but the square makes Us positive.

6 · Energy transfers

spring ↔ kinetic

For an ideal horizontal spring–cart system:

ΔK + ΔUs = 0

At maximum compression the cart may be momentarily at rest; at the relaxed position Us may be zero.

spring ↔ gravitational

For a spring launcher or vertical spring system:

ΔK + ΔUs + ΔUg = 0

Near Earth, use ΔUg = mgΔy.
