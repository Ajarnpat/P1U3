1. Start with your own idea

Energy is ...

Reveal the class note

Energy is a scalar quantity used to account for the state of an object or system and the changes that can occur. Energy can be transferred between systems and transformed between different stores/forms. Total energy is conserved.

Its SI unit is ...

Why joules?

The SI unit is the joule (J). One joule has base units kg m² s⁻².

Reveal GREENSICK and the bigger idea

Gravitational, Rotational, Elastic, Electrical, Nuclear, Sound, Internal, Chemical, Kinetic. It is a useful school-level memory aid, not a formal AP taxonomy. At a deeper level, many of these are combinations of kinetic and potential energies of particles or systems.

3. Kinetic or potential?

Broad idea: kinetic energy is associated with motion. Potential energy is associated with the configuration of an interacting system.

Predict the category. Click a card, then reveal the explanation.

Moving cart

Its center of mass is translating.

Category

Translational kinetic energy.

Spinning wheel

It rotates about an axis.

Category

Rotational kinetic energy.

Object-Earth system

The separation/height changes.

Category

Gravitational potential energy. Potential energy belongs to the system.

Compressed spring-block system

The spring is deformed.

Category

Elastic/spring potential energy.

Precision point: do not treat “potential energy” as something a lone object possesses. In AP reasoning, it is associated with positions/configurations of objects within a system.

5. Build the energy-equation toolkit

Translational kinetic energy

K = ½mv²

SI inputs: m in kg; v in m s⁻¹

When / why?

Current Unit 3 AP equation.

Rotational kinetic energy

KR = ½Iω²

SI inputs: I in kg m²; ω in rad s⁻¹

When / why?

AP equation developed formally in Unit 6; introduced here as part of the energy family.

Near-surface gravitational PE change

ΔUg = mgΔy

SI inputs: m in kg; g in N kg⁻¹; Δy in m

When / why?

Use when g is approximately constant.

General gravitational PE

Ug = −Gm₁m₂/r

SI inputs: G = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N m² kg⁻²; r in m

When / why?

Zero is defined at infinite separation.

Spring potential energy

Us = ½k(Δx)²

SI inputs: k in N m⁻¹; Δx in m

When / why?

Elastic energy stored in an ideal spring.

Internal-energy change

ΔUint = Uint,f − Uint,i

SI inputs: all energy values in J

When / why?

Useful bookkeeping notation. The current AP Physics 1 CED requires awareness of dissipation to thermal/sound but does not print a separate ΔU_int equation.

Mechanical energy

Emech = K + U

SI inputs: all terms in J

When / why?

The sum of kinetic and potential energies of the chosen system.

Mechanical energy: Emech = K + U. Add every kinetic and potential term that belongs to the chosen system.

6. Practice

Use the worksheet for your written working. In the HTML, open a Hint only after you have started; open the Worked solution only after a serious attempt.

A1Translational kinetic energy

A 2.0 kg cart moves at 3.0 m s⁻¹. Determine its translational kinetic energy.

Hint

Use K = ½mv². All quantities are already in SI units.

Worked solution
  1. K = ½mv²
  2. K = ½(2.0 kg)(3.0 m s⁻¹)²
  3. K = 9.0 J

Answer: 9.0 J

A2Rotational kinetic energy

A wheel has rotational inertia I = 0.50 kg m² and angular speed ω = 4.0 rad s⁻¹. Determine its rotational kinetic energy.

Hint

Use K_R = ½Iω².

Worked solution
  1. KR = ½Iω²
  2. KR = ½(0.50)(4.0)²
  3. KR = 4.0 J

Answer: 4.0 J

A3Gravitational PE change

A 3.0 kg object is raised vertically by 2.0 m near Earth. Use g = 9.8 N kg⁻¹. Determine ΔUg.

Hint

Use ΔU_g = mgΔy. Raising the object means Δy is positive.

Worked solution
  1. ΔUg = mgΔy
  2. ΔUg = (3.0)(9.8)(+2.0)
  3. ΔUg = +58.8 J

Answer: +58.8 J

A4General gravitational PE

A 1000 kg satellite is 7.00 × 10⁶ m from Earth’s center. Take Earth’s mass as 5.97 × 10²⁴ kg. Determine Ug.

Hint

Use U_g = −Gm₁m₂/r. Gravitational potential energy is negative with zero at infinite separation.

Worked solution
  1. Ug = −Gm₁m₂/r
  2. Ug = −(6.67 × 10⁻¹¹)(5.97 × 10²⁴)(1000)/(7.00 × 10⁶)
  3. Ug ≈ −5.69 × 10¹⁰ J

Answer: −5.69 × 10¹⁰ J

A5Spring potential energy

An ideal spring has k = 200 N m⁻¹ and is compressed by 0.10 m. Determine Us.

Hint

Use U_s = ½k(Δx)². The sign of Δx does not matter because it is squared.

Worked solution
  1. Us = ½k(Δx)²
  2. Us = ½(200)(0.10)²
  3. Us = 1.00 J

Answer: 1.00 J

A6Internal-energy change

A system’s internal energy changes from 250 J to 310 J. Determine ΔUint.

Hint

Change = final − initial.

Worked solution
  1. ΔUint = Uint,f − Uint,i
  2. ΔUint = 310 J − 250 J
  3. ΔUint = +60 J

Answer: +60 J

A7Mechanical energy

A system has K = 20 J, Ug = 35 J, and Us = 5 J. Determine Emech.

Hint

Mechanical energy is the sum of the kinetic and potential energies in the selected system.

Worked solution
  1. Emech = K + U
  2. Emech = 20 + 35 + 5
  3. Emech = 60 J

Answer: 60 J

B1Translational kinetic energy

A 750 g ball moves at 18 km h⁻¹. Determine K in joules.

Hint

Convert 750 g to kg and 18 km h⁻¹ to m s⁻¹ before using K = ½mv².

Worked solution
  1. m = 750 g = 0.750 kg
  2. v = 18 km h⁻¹ ÷ 3.6 = 5.0 m s⁻¹
  3. K = ½(0.750)(5.0)² = 9.375 J ≈ 9.4 J

Answer: 9.4 J

B2Rotational kinetic energy

A rotor has I = 0.020 kg m² and rotates at 120 rpm. Determine KR.

Hint

Convert rpm to rad s⁻¹: 120 rpm = 2 rev s⁻¹ = 4π rad s⁻¹.

Worked solution
  1. ω = 120 rev min⁻¹ × (1 min/60 s) × 2π rad/rev = 4π rad s⁻¹
  2. KR = ½Iω²
  3. KR = ½(0.020)(4π)² ≈ 1.58 J

Answer: 1.58 J

B3Gravitational PE change

A 650 g mass is lifted through 45 cm. Use g = 9.8 N kg⁻¹. Determine ΔUg.

Hint

Convert grams to kilograms and centimetres to metres.

Worked solution
  1. m = 0.650 kg; Δy = 0.45 m
  2. ΔUg = mgΔy
  3. ΔUg = (0.650)(9.8)(0.45) ≈ 2.87 J

Answer: 2.87 J

B4General gravitational PE

A 2.0 tonne satellite is 7000 km from Earth’s center. Use Earth mass 5.97 × 10²⁴ kg. Determine Ug.

Hint

2.0 tonnes = 2000 kg and 7000 km = 7.00 × 10⁶ m.

Worked solution
  1. m₂ = 2000 kg; r = 7.00 × 10⁶ m
  2. Ug = −Gm₁m₂/r
  3. Ug ≈ −1.14 × 10¹¹ J

Answer: −1.14 × 10¹¹ J

B5Spring potential energy

A spring has k = 3.0 N cm⁻¹ and is compressed by 6.0 cm. Determine Us.

Hint

Convert k: 1 N cm⁻¹ = 100 N m⁻¹. Convert 6.0 cm to 0.060 m.

Worked solution
  1. k = 3.0 N cm⁻¹ = 300 N m⁻¹; Δx = 0.060 m
  2. Us = ½k(Δx)²
  3. Us = ½(300)(0.060)² = 0.54 J

Answer: 0.54 J

B6Internal-energy change

A system’s internal energy changes from 0.45 kJ to 620 J. Determine ΔUint in joules.

Hint

Convert 0.45 kJ to joules first.

Worked solution
  1. Uint,i = 0.45 kJ = 450 J
  2. ΔUint = 620 − 450
  3. ΔUint = +170 J

Answer: +170 J

B7Mechanical energy

A system has K = 0.80 kJ, Ug = 350 J, and Us = 0.050 kJ. Determine Emech in joules.

Hint

Convert both kJ values to J, then add all kinetic and potential terms.

Worked solution
  1. K = 800 J; Us = 50 J
  2. Emech = 800 + 350 + 50
  3. Emech = 1200 J

Answer: 1200 J

C1Translational kinetic energy

An 8.0 kg object has K = 64 J. Determine its speed.

Hint

Rearrange K = ½mv² to v = √(2K/m).

Worked solution
  1. v = √(2K/m)
  2. v = √[(2)(64)/8.0]
  3. v = 4.0 m s⁻¹

Answer: 4.0 m s⁻¹

C2Rotational kinetic energy

A rotor has KR = 9.0 J and I = 0.50 kg m². Determine ω.

Hint

Rearrange K_R = ½Iω² to ω = √(2K_R/I).

Worked solution
  1. ω = √(2KR/I)
  2. ω = √[(2)(9.0)/0.50]
  3. ω = 6.0 rad s⁻¹

Answer: 6.0 rad s⁻¹

C3Gravitational PE change

A 5.0 kg object gains 196 J of gravitational potential energy near Earth. Use g = 9.8 N kg⁻¹. Determine Δy.

Hint

Rearrange ΔU_g = mgΔy.

Worked solution
  1. Δy = ΔUg/(mg)
  2. Δy = 196/[(5.0)(9.8)]
  3. Δy = 4.0 m

Answer: 4.0 m

C4General gravitational PE

A 1.00 × 10³ kg spacecraft has Ug = −6.67 × 10⁹ J in the field of a planet of mass 5.00 × 10²⁴ kg. Determine r.

Hint

Use the magnitude of U_g when rearranging: r = Gm₁m₂/|U_g|.

Worked solution
  1. r = Gm₁m₂/|Ug|
  2. r = (6.67 × 10⁻¹¹)(5.00 × 10²⁴)(1.00 × 10³)/(6.67 × 10⁹)
  3. r = 5.00 × 10⁷ m

Answer: 5.00 × 10⁷ m

C5Spring potential energy

A spring with k = 100 N m⁻¹ stores 4.5 J. Determine |Δx|.

Hint

Rearrange U_s = ½k(Δx)² to |Δx| = √(2U_s/k).

Worked solution
  1. |Δx| = √(2Us/k)
  2. |Δx| = √[(2)(4.5)/100]
  3. |Δx| = 0.30 m

Answer: 0.30 m

C6Internal-energy change

A system starts with Uint,i = 120 J and has ΔUint = +45 J. Determine Uint,f.

Hint

From ΔU_int = U_int,f − U_int,i, rearrange to U_int,f = U_int,i + ΔU_int.

Worked solution
  1. Uint,f = Uint,i + ΔUint
  2. Uint,f = 120 + 45
  3. Uint,f = 165 J

Answer: 165 J

C7Mechanical energy

A system has Emech = 90 J, K = 35 J, and Ug = 40 J. The only other mechanical-energy store is a spring. Determine Us.

Hint

Use E_mech = K + U_g + U_s and rearrange.

Worked solution
  1. Us = Emech − K − Ug
  2. Us = 90 − 35 − 40
  3. Us = 15 J

Answer: 15 J
